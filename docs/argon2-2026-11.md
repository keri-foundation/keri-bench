# One issuer after the argon2 fix, 20 November 2026

Every run since October has found one imbu issuer saturating at about 1.4 to 1.6 credentials a second, and three issuers at about 4. imbu-stamps' CPU profile explained why (`~/code/bakobo/imbu/.ignored/issuance-cpu-profile-2026-11.md`): 0.575 s of issuer CPU per credential, 0.49 s of it in argon2 `crypto_pwhash`, called once to blind the credential at issue and again to re-derive its disclosure at grant. Two heti changes followed. heti#74 lets a grant reuse the blinder heti kept at issue, so a credential pays argon2 once. heti#75 derives the issue-side blind on a process-wide pool of four threads, so concurrent issuances on one issuer stop queuing behind it on the event loop. imbu `7b544ac` (bakobo/imbu#49) pins the heti that carries both, `093acb3`.

This run asks how much that moved one issuer's capacity.

## The answer

One issuer at the new pin kept up with every rate offered, up to 4.0 a second, with no refusals and nothing lost. At the old pin it saturated at about 1.5 a second, as every earlier run had. The fix lifted one issuer's capacity at least 2.6 times, and the ceiling now lies somewhere above 4.0, which this run did not reach. The follow-ups the next night put it between 5 and 6 a second, and found one issuer narrowly failing the full peak hour for a reason the probes could not show.

| offered a second | old pin: witnessed a second | old pin: p50 requested → witnessed | new pin: witnessed a second | new pin: p50 / p99 requested → witnessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 1.00 | 0.40 s | 1.00 | 0.40 s / 0.55 s |
| 1.5 | 1.48 | 0.67 s | 1.50 | 0.39 s / 0.47 s |
| 2.0 | 1.54 | 10.0 s | 2.00 | 0.39 s / 0.52 s |
| 2.8 | 1.59 | 27.0 s | 2.82 | 0.41 s / 0.53 s |
| 3.5 | 1.45 | 31.9 s | 3.52 | 0.42 s / 0.70 s |
| 4.0 | 1.51 | 36.5 s | 4.00 | 0.43 s / 0.54 s |

At the old pin, from 2.8 a second up, credentials were lost: 4 of 140 at 2.8, 31 of 175 at 3.5 and 36 of 200 at 4.0, each refused or timed out. At 4.0 its issuer answered the adapter's sends with 2,540 of the answer budget's 429s and 565 of its 503 `e.env.saturated.r`. At the new pin, at 4.0, it answered none of either, and all 200 credentials requested were witnessed, one of them just after the window closed.

## Where the time goes

imbu's two new stamps, `constructed` and `registry_updated`, split what used to be one accepted → anchored stage. Medians, in milliseconds:

| offered a second | pin | requested → accepted | accepted → constructed | constructed → registry_updated | registry_updated → anchored | anchored → witnessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | old | 5 | 15 | 250 | 16 | 104 |
| 1.0 | new | 5 | 15 | 248 | 17 | 108 |
| 2.8 | old | 495 | 21,525 | 2,366 | 1,697 | 156 |
| 2.8 | new | 7 | 16 | 243 | 17 | 118 |
| 4.0 | old | 12,969 | 22,330 | 1,690 | 936 | 466 |
| 4.0 | new | 6 | 15 | 266 | 17 | 112 |

Below saturation the two pins are the same, and a credential's latency is dominated by constructed → registry_updated, about 0.25 s. That is one argon2 pass, which both pins pay on the latency path at issue, plus heti's wait for an earlier act to reach toad (imbu-stamps' description, which this run does not separate). So the fix does not make a single credential faster, and was not expected to. What it changes is how many credentials an issuer can carry at once. At the old pin, once offered load passed about 1.5 a second, the queue built in front of construction: accepted → constructed rose from 15 ms to 22 s, which is imbu's bounded backlog of about 30 s of work, and the rest was turned away. At the new pin every stage stayed at its unloaded value through 4.0 a second.

## What this does not show

The run cannot say how much of the gain each heti change bought. Halving argon2 alone would, by arithmetic from the profile, have lifted the CPU ceiling from about 1.75 to about 3 a second. The issuer passed 4.0, so the thread pool, which lets argon2 for concurrent credentials run on several cores at once, is plausibly most of the rest. Separating them would take a run at a heti with heti#74 and not heti#75.

Each rate is one 60 s window, a probe, and keeping up for a minute at 4.0 is not keeping up for an hour. The follow-ups below test that, find the new ceiling, and probe three issuers.

The old pin ran second. Each run's provenance records the host's one-minute load across its window: 1.9 to 4.7 at the new pin, rising with the offered rate, and 1.9 to 4.2 at the old pin, highest at its lowest rates and 1.9 to 3.1 at 2.8, 3.5 and 4.0. At those three rates the old pin's host was as busy as the new pin's or quieter (3.1 against 3.1, 2.6 against 2.6, 1.9 against 4.7), so a busier host does not explain why it saturated. Some of that load is the stack under test; the run cannot say how much came from other work on the host. `loadavg.log` on each side covers only the scenario's four rates, because `scripts/targets.sh` stops its logger before the two added ones, which is why the per-run figures here come from provenance.


## The follow-ups, 21 November

The same pin ran four follow-ups after midnight, from a load of 0.71, under the bench-host lock, with one calibration that passed the strict gate (`this.i` 3s6zgux4). Every run completed.

One issuer's ceiling is between 5 and 6 a second. At 5.0 it kept up, with p50 0.40 s and p99 0.58 s from request to witnessed. At 6.0 it witnessed 4.66 a second inside the window and p99 rose to 14.7 s, though every credential was witnessed by the end of the drain. At 8.0 the queue built in front of construction, accepted → constructed reaching 10.7 s at the median, and it witnessed 6.0 a second. The host's load across the window rose from 1.7 at 5.0 to 7.8 at 8.0, so at 8.0 the eight cores were close to busy, and the run cannot say whether the ceiling is imbu's or the host's.

Three issuers kept up at every rate offered, up to 12.0 a second, with p50 0.55 s and p99 0.82 s at 12.0 and nothing refused. Their ceiling lies above 12.0, which this run did not reach. The worry that four argon2 threads per issuer would make three issuers contend for this host's cores did not show at these rates; the load across the window at 12.0 was 4.0.

One issuer passed the ten-minute peak hour, `peak-hour-1home`, which it had failed from its first minute on every earlier pin: all 1,680 credentials witnessed at 2.8 a second, p50 0.37 s and p99 0.48 s.

One issuer narrowly failed the full peak hour, `peak-hour-1home-hour`, which asks for 10,000 credentials witnessed at 2.8 a second within the hour. It witnessed 9,877 in the hour, 2.74 a second. Of the 10,080 requested, 201 were never witnessed; most were given up after their 60 s operation timeout. For most of the hour every bucket of 200 credentials had p50 0.36 to 0.43 s and p99 under 0.6 s. The failures came in three stalls, starting about 16, 31 and 46 minutes into the hour, each affecting two to three minutes of requests:

| credentials requested | p50 requested → witnessed | p99 | not witnessed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2,600 to 3,000 | 23 to 24 s | 54 to 80 s | 1 |
| 5,200 to 5,600 | 32 to 44 s | 68 to 91 s | 75 |
| 7,600 to 8,000 | 0.4 to 50 s | 71 to 89 s | 117 |

The other 8 of the 201 were requested in the buckets on either side of these.

Each stall is in accepted → constructed, whose median went from 0.01 s to 20 to 32 s in the worst minute of each stall before recovering; the later stages slowed far less. The stalls come every 900 s, which is the Registrar window this scenario deploys. During the first two the stack's stores grew by up to 47 MB a minute, against about 2 MB a minute otherwise. While construction was stalled the issuer's backlog bound and answer budget turned requests away: across the hour it answered 1,810 503s and 10,645 429s. I have not established what imbu does at the window that holds construction. The correlation with the window is strong, and the cause is a question for imbu. At three issuers each takes a third of the load, which is plausibly why the three-issuer peak hour, which uses the same window, passed in `docs/targets-2026-10-rerun.md`.

Revocation in the same run was slow: of the 97 credentials taken through the whole lifecycle, 68 revocations were recognised and 29 failed, 12 of them after the hour had ended. Revocation requested → anchored had p50 21 s. This bears on target 3 at one issuer, which nothing earlier measured.

So on this pin one issuer carries the peak-hour rate for as long as it is not interrupted, and loses about 2% of an hour to the stall at each Registrar window. Three issuers carry four times the peak hour's rate in a one-minute probe.

The runs are in `results/argon2-followups-2026-11`, with the driver script `run.sh` and its log `run.log`.

## How it was run

On kelun, on 20 November 2026, under the shared `~/.local/state/bench-host.lock`, starting once the one-minute load fell under 2 (it was 1.40). `probe-capacity-1home` ran at its four rates through `scripts/targets.sh`, then at 3.5 and 4.0 with `keri-bench run --rate`, first at the new pin, harness `81c2ca5`, imbu `7b544ac`, heti `093acb3`, and then at the old pin, harness `1d59ce2`, imbu `317ae49`, heti `12e6f41`. Each side calibrated first under the strict gate and passed. Why it was run this way is `this.i` 279zu9ps, under the pin move 06klkn9m.

The runs are in `results/argon2-2026-11`, `after/` for the new pin and `before/` for the old, with the driver script `run.sh` and its log `run.log`.
